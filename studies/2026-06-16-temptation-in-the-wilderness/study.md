# Jesus and Me: Tempted in the Wilderness

**Date:** June 16, 2026  
**Passages:** Matthew 4:1-10; Deuteronomy 6:16  
**Translation:** CSB  
**Type:** Teaching Outline Study

---

## Overview

In the account of Jesus' temptation in the wilderness, Satan attacks not merely to make Jesus sin, but to tempt him to abandon trust in the Father and compromise God's established standards. These same three temptations — independence from God, presumption upon God, and taking shortcuts around God's way — confront every believer today. Jesus' victory through Scripture and dependence on the Father models how we resist.

## Central Question

**Question:** What are some of the ways that we put God to the test ("tempt") the Lord by our actions?

**Answer:** To tempt God is to presume upon his character, to test his faithfulness, or to demand that he conform to our expectations rather than trusting his revealed will. It is the opposite of faith — it manipulates rather than trusts, demands rather than submits, and experiments rather than obeys. Deuteronomy 6:16 commands, "You shall not test the LORD your God," a warning Jesus quoted when Satan urged presumption. We tempt God when we take foolish risks expecting divine rescue, demand miraculous signs before obeying clear commands, live in deliberate sin while presuming on his protection, treat prayer like a bargaining challenge, or presume upon his grace as license for recklessness.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Deuteronomy 6:16** (CSB)
   > Do not test the LORD your God as you tested him at Massah.
   
   **Word Study:** *Nasah* (נָסָה) — "to test, try, prove" — implies putting God on trial, demanding proof of his faithfulness rather than trusting his word. At Massah (Exodus 17:7), Israel demanded, "Is the LORD among us or not?" despite his daily provision of manna. This word carries the sense of a suspicious test, not faith-filled dependence.

2. **Malachi 3:15** (CSB)
   > So now we consider the arrogant to be fortunate. Not only do those who commit wickedness prosper, they even test God and escape.
   
   **Word Study:** *Bachan* (בָּחַן) — "to test, examine, try" — used here of the wicked who deliberately sin and dare God to judge them. They live as if God's warnings are negotiable, his holiness optional. This is the heart of tempting God: living as though his character is uncertain and must be proven on our terms.

3. **Acts 5:9** (CSB)
   > Then Peter said to her, "Why did you agree to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who have buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out."
   
   **Word Study:** *Peirazō* (πειράζω) — "to test, tempt, put to the trial" — Ananias and Sapphira conspired to lie to the Holy Spirit, treating God as though he could be deceived. They presumed on his patience and tested whether judgment would really come. Presumption is a form of practical atheism.

**Practical Application:** This week, identify one area where you've been demanding a "sign" before obeying God's clear command (e.g., waiting for "peace" about generosity when Scripture already commands it, or demanding financial certainty before tithing). Choose to obey the command without requiring extra proof that God will come through.

---

**Question:** What does faith look like instead?

**Answer:** Faith looks like obedience before clarity, trust without miraculous signs, and dependence on God's word rather than our assessment of circumstances. Faith does not demand proof or test God's faithfulness — it rests on his revealed character and obeys his clear commands even when the outcome is uncertain. Jesus modeled this perfectly in the wilderness: hungry, physically weakened, and under Satanic assault, he did not use his divine power independently but trusted the Father's provision and timing. Faith says, "God has spoken; I will obey," while presumption says, "I will act recklessly and see if God rescues me." Faith works within God's means; presumption expects God to override our foolishness.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Hebrews 11:1, 6** (CSB)
   > Now faith is the reality of what is hoped for, the proof of what is not seen... Now without faith it is impossible to please God, since the one who draws near to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.
   
   **Word Study:** *Hypostasis* (ὑπόστασις) — "substance, reality, assurance" — faith is not wishful thinking but confident assurance grounded in God's promises. It does not require visible proof because it trusts the unseen God. *Pistis* (πίστις), "faith," is the opposite of testing God; it is settled confidence that God is who he says he is.

2. **Proverbs 3:5-6** (CSB)
   > Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not rely on your own understanding; in all your ways know him, and he will make your paths straight.
   
   **Word Study:** *Batach* (בָּטַח) — "to trust, be confident, feel secure" — this word implies leaning your full weight on something, not cautiously testing it. Faith does not lean partly on God and partly on our own schemes. The parallel command, "do not rely (*sha'an*, lean on) your own understanding," shows faith is wholehearted dependence on God's wisdom, not ours.

3. **2 Corinthians 5:7** (CSB)
   > For we walk by faith, not by sight.
   
   **Word Study:** *Eidos* (εἶδος) — "appearance, sight, what is visible" — faith does not require visible confirmation before trusting. It obeys what God has said even when circumstances scream otherwise. Paul walked this out in suffering, imprisonment, and weakness, trusting God's unseen purposes rather than demanding miraculous rescue.

**Practical Application:** This week, choose one Scripture command you've been hesitating on (e.g., forgiving someone, addressing a sin, giving sacrificially) and obey it immediately without waiting for feelings to align or circumstances to improve. Let obedience precede clarity.

## Three Temptations: A Deeper Dive

### Temptation #1: "Command These Stones to Become Bread" (4:3)

**The Temptation:** Use your power (your abilities, resources, talents) independently of the Father to meet your own needs. Satan tempted Jesus to act apart from dependence on the Father — to solve the hunger problem on his own terms and timing rather than trusting the Father's provision. This is the temptation to self-sufficiency, to trust our own abilities more than God's word and timing.

**Modern Examples:**
- Sacrificing family time, worship, or rest for financial gain — trusting our hustle more than God's provision
- Compromising integrity to get ahead in career or business — the end justifies the means
- Letting the "easy way" or the "practical solution" determine obedience rather than Scripture
- Using manipulation or control to get what we want rather than praying and trusting
- Functional atheism: living as though success depends entirely on our effort, not God's blessing

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Deuteronomy 8:3** (CSB) — Jesus' response
   > He humbled you by letting you go hungry; then he gave you manna to eat, which you and your ancestors had not known, so that you might learn that man does not live on bread alone but on every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD.
   
   **Word Study:** *Davar* (דָּבָר) — "word, matter, thing" — not just Scripture, but every word God speaks, including his commands, his promises, his timing. Jesus was saying, "The Father's word and will are more essential than physical food. I will not act independently to meet my needs; I trust his provision and timing."

2. **Proverbs 3:5-7** (CSB)
   > Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not rely on your own understanding; in all your ways know him, and he will make your paths straight. Don't be wise in your own eyes; fear the LORD and turn away from evil.
   
   **Word Study:** *Biyn* (בִּין) — "understanding, discernment" — human wisdom and self-assessment. The warning is not to lean on our own calculations of what's needed or what will work. Faith trusts God's wisdom over our own strategic planning.

3. **Matthew 6:31-33** (CSB)
   > So don't worry, saying, 'What will we eat?' or 'What will we drink?' or 'What will we wear?' For the Gentiles eagerly seek all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them. But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be provided for you.
   
   **Word Study:** *Prostatithēmi* (προστίθημι) — "to add, to provide in addition" — God promises to add provision when we seek his kingdom first. The temptation is to reverse the order: seek provision first, then fit kingdom priorities around it. Jesus models kingdom-first trust.

**Practical Application:** Identify one decision you're facing this week where you're tempted to compromise obedience for the "practical" solution (e.g., skipping church for work, cutting ethical corners for financial gain, manipulating a relationship to get your way). Choose the God-honoring path and journal one prayer entrusting the outcome to him.

*Trust in God's provision does not mean passivity; it means working hard within God's ways, not cutting corners around them.*

---

### Temptation #2: "Throw Yourself Down" (4:5-7)

**The Temptation:** Expect God to rescue us from foolish, self-chosen risks. Satan quotes Psalm 91:11-12 to tempt Jesus to jump from the temple and force the Father to send angels — a spectacular, public miracle proving Jesus' identity. But this is presumption, not faith. It is taking a reckless risk and demanding God bail us out. Faith obeys God's commands; presumption tests God's promises.

**Modern Examples:**
- Making foolish financial decisions (debt, speculation, living beyond means) and expecting God to cover the consequences
- Entering relationships, marriages, or partnerships against biblical wisdom and praying for blessing
- Neglecting health, rest, or stewardship and expecting God to heal or provide supernaturally
- Treating God's promises like a magic safety net that allows recklessness
- Spiritual foolishness: living in unrepentant sin while claiming God's protection and grace

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Deuteronomy 6:16** (CSB) — Jesus' response
   > Do not test the LORD your God as you tested him at Massah.
   
   **Word Study:** *Massah* (מַסָּה) — "testing" — the place where Israel doubted God's presence and provision (Exodus 17:1-7). They demanded proof: "Is the LORD among us or not?" Jesus refused to put the Father on trial. Presumption says, "I'll take the risk and see if God is faithful." Faith says, "God has already proven himself; I will walk in his ways."

2. **Psalm 91:11-12** (CSB) — The passage Satan misused
   > For he will give his angels orders concerning you, to protect you in all your ways. They will support you with their hands so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.
   
   **Word Study:** *Shamar* (שָׁמַר) — "to keep, guard, protect" — God promises protection **in all your ways** — that is, in the paths of obedience and trust, not in reckless self-chosen dangers. Satan omitted the context (Psalm 91:9-10), which promises protection for "the one who lives under the protection of the Most High." Presumption twists promises out of context.

3. **James 4:13-15** (CSB)
   > Come now, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will travel to such and such a city and spend a year there and do business and make a profit." Yet you do not know what tomorrow will bring—what your life will be! For you are like vapor that appears for a little while, then vanishes. Instead, you should say, "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that."
   
   **Word Study:** *Thelō* (θέλω) — "to will, to desire, to purpose" — God's sovereign will. James rebukes presumption: assuming we control outcomes and can plan independently of God's will. Faith holds plans loosely, submitting to God's sovereign purposes.

**Practical Application:** This week, identify one area where you've been presuming on God's grace — taking a foolish risk (financial, relational, moral) and expecting him to rescue you from consequences. Confess it as presumption, not faith, and make a concrete change: pay down debt, set a boundary, flee temptation, or seek wise counsel before acting.

*Faith trusts God's protection within obedience; presumption tests God's patience outside it.*

---

### Temptation #3: "All These Kingdoms I Will Give You" (4:8-9)

**The Temptation:** Get what you want without doing it God's way. Satan offers Jesus the kingdoms of the world — his rightful inheritance as Messiah — but without the cross, without suffering, without submission to the Father's plan. Just bow to Satan and take the shortcut. This is the temptation to achieve good ends through evil means, to gain the world through compromise, to get God's promises without God's process.

**Modern Examples:**
- Success without integrity
- Wealth without honesty
- Influence without humility
- Pleasure without obedience
- Ministry impact through manipulation or self-promotion rather than faithfulness
- Marital happiness through selfishness rather than sacrificial love
- Parenting "success" through control and fear rather than gospel-centered shepherding

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Deuteronomy 6:13** (CSB) — Jesus' response
   > Fear the LORD your God, worship him, and take your oaths in his name.
   
   **Word Study:** *Yare* (יָרֵא) — "to fear, revere, stand in awe" — the fear of the LORD is the foundation of wisdom and obedience. Worship belongs to God alone. Satan's offer required Jesus to worship him — to acknowledge Satan as the source of blessing. Every shortcut around God's way is a form of bowing to another lord: self, money, power, comfort, reputation.

2. **Matthew 16:26** (CSB)
   > For what will it benefit someone if he gains the whole world yet loses his life? Or what will anyone give in exchange for his life?
   
   **Word Study:** *Kerdainō* (κερδαίνω) — "to gain, profit, win" — the ironic tragedy of gaining everything but losing your soul. Shortcuts promise gain but deliver destruction. Jesus refused the kingdoms through Satan's way because the Father's way — the cross, resurrection, and exaltation — would win the nations righteously and eternally.

3. **Proverbs 14:12** (CSB)
   > There is a way that seems right to a person, but its end is the way to death.
   
   **Word Study:** *Yashar* (יָשָׁר) — "right, straight, upright" — the path that **seems** right to human wisdom. The world's shortcuts look efficient, practical, even wise, but they lead to death. God's way — integrity, honesty, humility, obedience — looks costly, slow, foolish, but it leads to life.

**Practical Application:** This week, identify one area where you're tempted to compromise God's way for faster results: cutting ethical corners at work, manipulating a relationship to get your way, using fear or anger to control your kids, or pursuing pleasure outside God's design. Write down the "shortcut" you're tempted by and beside it write, "The way that seems right leads to death. I will worship God alone by doing this his way." Then take one concrete step of obedience in that area.

*God's ways lead to life, even when they look costly; the world's shortcuts lead to death, even when they look easy.*

## Simple Summary

**Jesus defeated Satan not by using his divine power independently, but by trusting the Father's word and obeying it completely.** We face the same three temptations: independence from God (self-sufficiency), presumption upon God (testing his faithfulness), and shortcuts around God (compromise for quick gain). Faith does not demand proof, take reckless risks, or compromise obedience for convenience. Faith trusts God's provision, walks in his ways, and worships him alone — even when it's costly, slow, and looks foolish to the world.

*Obey the word. Trust the Father. Worship God alone.*
