# Colossians 3 - 2026-06-02

*A topical / teaching-outline study walking through Colossians 3 section by section.*

## Intro

In Colossians 2, Paul dismantled the false teaching threatening the church—the hollow philosophy, the legalism, the worship of angels. He made it clear: Christ is enough. You died with him; the old regulations and spiritual powers have no claim on you anymore.

But chapter 3 is where Paul answers the next question: if we're free from all that, what do we do now? How do we actually live out this new identity? Paul doesn't leave us in abstract theology. He gives us the blueprint—mind-recalibration, sin-mortification, new-self clothing, and household faithfulness. This is comprehensive Christian discipleship grounded in union with Christ.

## Anchor Text

**Colossians 3:1–25** (CSB)

The entire chapter is our anchor tonight. Paul moves through four movements:

1. **Verses 1–3** — Seek things above (the foundation)
2. **Verses 5–9** — Put to death what is earthly (mortification)
3. **Verses 12–17** — Put on the new self (clothing ourselves in Christ-likeness)
4. **Verses 18–25** — Instructions for Christian households (lived-out faith in family and work)

Each movement depends on the one before it. You can't "put on" virtue if you haven't first "put to death" sin. And you can't do either without first recalibrating your mind to the realities of heaven.

## Q1. Can you give examples of what this looks like in your family, at work, and your social activities?

**Answer.** Paul anchors everything in verse 1: "So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things above." This is not escapism—it's reorientation. The "things above" are the *realities* of the gospel: Christ is risen, seated at God's right hand, reigning now, and my life is hidden with him. When I set my mind there, my real life is no longer the scramble for approval at work, the quest for comfort at home, or the drift toward entertainment in my downtime. My real life is bound up with Christ's. That changes the questions I ask and the values I carry into every room.

In the family, this looks like leading Erin and the kids toward worship and scripture, not just managing logistics. It means my patience with Lucy's math homework isn't powered by "I should be a good dad" but by "I've been raised with Christ; his patience fuels mine." At work, I'm not building software to secure my reputation or pad my comp—I'm stewarding the skills God gave me, working for an audience of One. In social settings, I'm not performing to be liked or hiding to be safe—I'm free to speak truth, love well, and let the chips fall, because my real life isn't up for a vote.

The key is Paul's phrase "your life is hidden with Christ in God" (v. 3). My true self—my identity, my security, my future—is locked away in the most secure vault in the universe. No boss, no spouse, no friend, no algorithm can touch it. That's not theory. That's the operating system upgrade Paul is calling for.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Colossians 3:1-3** (CSB)
   > So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. Set your minds on things above, not on earthly things. For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.

2. **Romans 6:11-12** (CSB)
   > So, you too consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus. Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires.

3. **Philippians 3:20** (CSB)
   > Our citizenship is in heaven, and we eagerly wait for a Savior from there, the Lord Jesus Christ.

**Word Studies:**

- **Greek: *phronēo* (φρονέω)** — "to set the mind on, to think, to be intent on." This is not casual daydreaming. It's the orientation of your whole thought life. Paul uses it twice in v. 2: "set your minds on things above, not on earthly things." Your default mental posture—where your mind drifts when you're not forcing it elsewhere—reveals what you actually treasure.

- **Greek: *zēteō* (ζητέω)** — "to seek, to strive for, to pursue earnestly." The word implies active pursuit, not passive hoping. "Seek the things above" is a call to reorder your priorities, your calendar, and your ambitions around the realities of Christ's reign, not the shadows of this world.

- **Greek: *kryptō* (κρύπτω)** — "to hide, to conceal, to keep secret." Your life is "hidden with Christ in God" (v. 3). This is not obscurity but security. The world cannot see your true life because it's stored in God himself. You're no longer exposed to the tyranny of reputation or the verdict of the crowd.

**Personal Application:** This week, before I start work each morning, I'll read Colossians 3:1-3 out loud at my desk and then write one sentence answering: "What would change today if I truly believed my real life is hidden with Christ?" Do this every day for seven days and notice which patterns shift—email tone, task prioritization, patience with interruptions.

## Q2. What are some of the things of this world that we must rid from our lives?

**Answer.** Paul gives two lists in verses 5–9, and they're not random. The first list (v. 5) is about disordered desires—sexual immorality, impurity, lust, evil desire, and greed. The second list (v. 8) is about relational destruction—anger, wrath, malice, slander, filthy language, and lying. Notice the pattern: sin that starts in my internal world (what I crave) spills out into the external world (how I treat people).

Paul calls greed "idolatry" (v. 5). That's the diagnostic. Idolatry is when I look to something other than God to give me what only God can give—security, satisfaction, identity, worth. Greed says, "More will finally be enough." Lust says, "That person will finally satisfy." Anger says, "I deserve better than this." All of it is worship of self or circumstance instead of Christ.

Verses 6–7 are the sobering reality check: "Because of these, God's wrath is coming upon the disobedient, and you once walked in these things when you were living in them." Past tense. Paul is saying, "This used to be your life. But you died with Christ. These things no longer define you." The fight to put them away is not about *becoming* someone new—it's about *living consistently with who you already are* in Christ.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Colossians 3:5-9** (CSB)
   > Therefore, put to death what belongs to your earthly nature: sexual immorality, impurity, lust, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry. Because of these, God's wrath is coming upon the disobedient, and you once walked in these things when you were living in them. But now, put away all the following: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and filthy language from your mouth. Do not lie to one another, since you have put off the old self with its practices.

2. **Galatians 5:24** (CSB)
   > Now those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.

3. **James 1:14-15** (CSB)
   > But each person is tempted when he is drawn away and enticed by his own evil desire. Then after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin, and when sin is fully grown, it gives birth to death.

**Word Studies:**

- **Greek: *pleonexia* (πλεονεξία)** — "greed, covetousness, desire for more." Literally "the desire to have more." Paul equates this directly with idolatry. The person who always wants more—more money, more status, more comfort—has made an idol of accumulation. It's never enough because the thing itself was never meant to satisfy.

- **Greek: *apothēmi* (ἀποτίθημι)** — "to put away, to lay aside, to take off (like clothing)." Paul uses this in v. 8: "put away all the following." The image is deliberate—these sins are like filthy garments you wore in your old life. You don't clean them up; you strip them off and burn them.

- **Greek: *orgē* (ὀργή)** — "wrath, anger." Distinguished from *thymos* (explosive rage). *Orgē* is the settled, simmering anger that nurses a grudge. It's the bitterness I carry into tomorrow because I won't let today go. Paul says: take it off. It doesn't fit who you are now.

**Personal Application:** I'll do an honest audit this week. Open my phone's browser history, my YouTube recommendations, the last five Slack conversations where I got heated. Ask: which of Paul's two lists shows up most—disordered desire or relational toxicity? Confess the specific pattern to Erin out loud, name it as the old-self garment it is, and ask her to hold me accountable when she sees it resurfacing.

## Q3. How do we "put to death" the sinful, earthly things lurking within us?

**Answer.** Paul's command is surgical: "put to death what belongs to your earthly nature" (v. 5). The Greek word is *nekroō*—to make dead, to deprive of power. This is not suppression or management. This is execution. But how?

First, you have to believe verse 3: "you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God." The cutting has already happened. At the cross, my old self—the one enslaved to sin—was put to death with Christ. The fight now is not to *become* dead to sin; it's to *live consistently* with the death that already occurred. This is why Paul says "consider yourselves dead to sin" (Rom 6:11). It's a done deal. My job is to treat it that way.

Practically, mortification means starving sin of the oxygen it needs. Lust feeds on unguarded eyes—so I put guardrails on my phone and laptop. Greed feeds on comparison—so I stop scrolling feeds designed to make me feel behind. Anger feeds on rehearsing offenses—so I interrupt the mental replay and pray instead. You can't white-knuckle your way to holiness, but you *can* cut off the supply lines.

And here's the gospel anchor: I don't do this to *earn* God's favor. I do it *because I already have it*. Christ's love is the fuel, not the reward. The man who tries to kill sin out of self-improvement will burn out. The man who kills sin because he's already beloved, already secure, already seated with Christ—he has the power to fight.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Colossians 3:5** (CSB)
   > Therefore, put to death what belongs to your earthly nature: sexual immorality, impurity, lust, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry.

2. **Romans 8:13** (CSB)
   > For if you live according to the flesh, you are going to die. But if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.

3. **Ephesians 4:22-24** (CSB)
   > to take off your former way of life, the old self that is corrupted by deceitful desires, to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to put on the new self, the one created according to God's likeness in righteousness and purity of the truth.

**Word Studies:**

- **Greek: *nekroō* (νεκρόω)** — "to put to death, to make dead, to deprive of power." This is the word in v. 5. It's the same root as *nekros* (dead body, corpse). Paul is not asking for sin management—he's demanding execution. Sin must be rendered powerless, starved, and left for dead.

- **Greek: *melos* (μέλος)** — "members, parts, limbs." Paul uses this in Romans 6:13 when he says "do not offer any parts of it to sin." Your eyes, hands, mouth, mind—these are the "members" through which sin gains ground. Mortification is practical: which member needs to be brought under Christ's lordship this week?

- **Greek: *palaios anthrōpos* (παλαιὸς ἄνθρωπος)** — "the old man, the old self." Paul says in v. 9 "you have put off the old self." This is not the self you're stuck with. This is the self crucified with Christ. The man you used to be—the one ruled by sin—is dead. You're fighting from victory, not for it.

**Personal Application:** This week, I'll identify one sin supply line I've been tolerating and cut it off. If it's lust, I'll move my phone out of the bedroom and set Screen Time restrictions on my laptop. If it's anger, I'll write down the offense I keep replaying and then burn the paper and pray Colossians 3:13 over the person. Concrete action, this week, named and done.

## Q4. How do we develop these qualities? ("clothe ourselves")

**Answer.** Verses 12–17 are the wardrobe change. Paul just told us to strip off the filthy garments of the old life; now he tells us what to put on: compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, forgiveness, and above all, love. But notice the order Paul uses: "Therefore, as God's chosen ones, holy and dearly loved, put on…" (v. 12). The indicative precedes the imperative. You *are* chosen, holy, dearly loved—*therefore* clothe yourself accordingly.

This is not "fake it till you make it." This is "live consistently with who you already are." When I put on compassion, I'm not manufacturing a feeling—I'm reflecting the compassion Christ showed me. When I forgive, I'm not gritting my teeth and releasing a debt—I'm echoing the forgiveness I've already received (v. 13). The Christian life is not behavior modification. It's identity-alignment.

The question asks *how* we develop these qualities. Answer: immersion. Verse 16 says "Let the word of Christ dwell richly among you." The word *dwell* means to make a home, to settle in permanently. You develop Christlikeness the same way you develop fluency in a language—immersion. Saturate yourself in scripture. Surround yourself with brothers who call you higher. Sing the gospel to yourself when the old patterns resurface. The word does the work; your job is to let it in.

And v. 17 is the capstone: "And whatever you do, in word or in deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him." Everything. Email tone, bedtime routines, code reviews, date nights. All of it under Christ's name, fueled by gratitude for what he's already done.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Colossians 3:12-14** (CSB)
   > Therefore, as God's chosen ones, holy and dearly loved, put on compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience, bearing with one another and forgiving one another if anyone has a grievance against another. Just as the Lord has forgiven you, so you are also to forgive. Above all, put on love, which is the perfect bond of unity.

2. **Ephesians 4:23-24** (CSB)
   > to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to put on the new self, the one created according to God's likeness in righteousness and purity of the truth.

3. **2 Peter 1:3-4** (CSB)
   > His divine power has given us everything required for life and godliness through the knowledge of him who called us by his own glory and goodness. By these he has given us very great and precious promises, so that through them you may share in the divine nature, escaping the corruption that is in the world because of evil desire.

**Word Studies:**

- **Greek: *enduō* (ἐνδύω)** — "to put on, to clothe oneself, to wear." Paul uses this word repeatedly in the New Testament to describe the Christian life as putting on Christ (Rom 13:14), the new self (Eph 4:24), and here, the virtues of Christ. The image is deliberate: you chose what you wear. You can't change your height, but you *can* clothe yourself. Paul says: dress like who you are—chosen, holy, beloved.

- **Greek: *enoikeō* (ἐνοικέω)** — "to dwell in, to live in, to make one's home." This is v. 16: "Let the word of Christ dwell richly among you." Not "visit occasionally" or "make a cameo." *Dwell.* The word must have permanent residence in your heart, your conversations, your thought patterns. Immersion, not information.

- **Greek: *agapē* (ἀγάπη)** — "love, covenant love." Paul says "above all, put on love, which is the perfect bond of unity" (v. 14). *Agapē* is not affection or sentiment—it's the decision to seek someone else's good regardless of cost. It's Christ-love, the love that chose the cross. This is the garment that holds everything else together.

**Personal Application:** This week, I'll pick one virtue from v. 12 that feels least natural to me—likely patience—and ask Erin and the kids to call me out when they see the opposite. Then, when they do, I'll stop, thank them, pray Colossians 3:12 out loud in front of them, and try again. Let the family be the gym where I build the muscle.

## Q5. How do we get our wives to "submit" to our leadership?

**Answer.** The short answer: you don't. Verse 18 says "Wives, submit yourselves to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord." Paul speaks to wives directly—not to husbands about their wives. That's not a loophole; it's the design. Submission is her calling before God, not my weapon to demand compliance.

But here's the deeper question: why would a wife *want* to submit to her husband? Answer: when submission looks like following Christ, not following a fool. If my leadership is marked by love, sacrifice, and Christ-likeness (which is exactly what Paul will demand of husbands in Ephesians 5:25–27), then submission becomes a rational, joyful response, not coerced obedience.

Paul's instruction to husbands in v. 19 is surgical: "love your wives and don't be bitter toward them." The Greek word for "bitter" (*pikrainō*) means to embitter, to make harsh, to provoke resentment. Most failed leadership in marriage isn't dramatic—it's death by a thousand cuts. Sarcasm. Dismissiveness. Neglect. Unilateral decision-making. Emotional distance. That's what embitters a wife. And when she's embittered, she's not following you to the grocery store, much less into spiritual leadership.

The model is always Christ and the church. He leads by laying down his life. He washes feet before he gives commands. If I want Erin to trust my leadership, I earn it the same way: by serving her well, listening to her counsel, protecting her dignity, and never using my role as a club.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Colossians 3:18-19** (CSB)
   > Wives, submit yourselves to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord. Husbands, love your wives and don't be bitter toward them.

2. **Ephesians 5:25-27** (CSB)
   > Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself for her to make her holy, cleansing her with the washing of water by the word. He did this to present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or anything like that, but holy and blameless.

3. **1 Peter 3:7** (CSB)
   > Husbands, in the same way, live with your wives in an understanding way, as with a weaker partner, showing them honor as coheirs of the grace of life, so that your prayers will not be hindered.

**Word Studies:**

- **Greek: *hypotassō* (ὑποτάσσω)** — "to submit, to place under, to arrange under." It's a military term—voluntary alignment under authority. Paul uses the middle voice here ("submit *yourselves*"), meaning the wife does this willingly, not because she's forced. The husband's job is to make that safe and wise by leading like Christ.

- **Greek: *pikrainō* (πικραίνω)** — "to embitter, to make bitter, to provoke resentment." This is v. 19. The husband who is harsh, dismissive, or neglectful provokes bitterness. Paul's warning is clear: love, don't corrode. Your leadership is not command-and-control; it's sacrifice and service.

- **Greek: *agapaō* (ἀγαπάω)** — "to love with covenant love." This is the verb form of *agapē*. Paul commands husbands to love their wives the way Christ loved the church—total, sacrificial, death-defying love. Not "I love you when you please me" but "I love you because I've decided to, and nothing will change that."

**Personal Application:** This week, I'll ask Erin one question every night before bed: "Did I do or say anything today that felt harsh or dismissive?" And when she answers honestly, I will not defend myself—I'll listen, apologize if needed, and ask how I can do better tomorrow. Repeat for seven days and notice what changes.

## Q6. How do we relate to verse 22?

**Answer.** Verse 22 says "Slaves, obey your human masters in everything. Don't work only while being watched, as people-pleasers, but work wholeheartedly, fearing the Lord." The word "slaves" stops us cold—and rightly so. Paul is not endorsing the institution of slavery. He's addressing Christians who found themselves in that brutal reality and needed to know how to live faithfully within it while the gospel did its slow, subversive work to dismantle it.

But here's the translation for us in 2026: *workplace authority*. I don't have a "master" in the Roman sense, but I do have a boss, a client, a project lead. And Paul's principle applies: don't work just to be seen (v. 22), and don't clock in for a paycheck alone. Work "from the heart, as something done for the Lord and not for people" (v. 23). My real employer is Christ. My manager is just the intermediary.

This changes everything. When I write code, I'm not performing for my tech lead's approval—I'm stewarding the skill God gave me, for his glory. When I hit a deadline crunch, I'm not grinding to avoid shame—I'm serving Christ by serving my team well. And when I get passed over for promotion or credit, I don't spiral, because my real reward is "the inheritance from the Lord" (v. 24), not the LinkedIn endorsement.

Verse 25 is the gut-check: "For the wrongdoer will be paid back for whatever wrong he has done, and there is no favoritism." That cuts both ways. The unjust boss will answer to God. And so will I, if I slack off, cut corners, or phone it in. Paul is leveling the playing field—no one escapes accountability. That makes work sacred, not because my boss is watching, but because Christ is.

**Biblical Support:**

1. **Colossians 3:22-24** (CSB)
   > Slaves, obey your human masters in everything. Don't work only while being watched, as people-pleasers, but work wholeheartedly, fearing the Lord. Whatever you do, do it from the heart, as something done for the Lord and not for people, knowing that you will receive the reward of an inheritance from the Lord. You serve the Lord Christ.

2. **Ephesians 6:5-8** (CSB)
   > Slaves, obey your human masters with fear and trembling, in the sincerity of your heart, as to Christ. Don't work only while being watched, as people-pleasers, but as slaves of Christ, do God's will from your heart. Serve with a good attitude, as to the Lord and not to people, knowing that the Lord will reward each one for whatever good he does—whether he is a slave or free.

3. **1 Corinthians 10:31** (CSB)
   > So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God.

**Word Studies:**

- **Greek: *ophthalmodouleō* (ὀφθαλμοδουλέω)** — "eye-service, work done only when watched." This is a rare compound word Paul uses in v. 22: *ophthalmos* (eye) + *douleō* (to serve as a slave). It's the guy who works hard when the boss walks by and slacks when he doesn't. Paul calls it out as people-pleasing. Performance theater. The Christian works the same in public and in private because the real audience never looks away.

- **Greek: *psychē* (ψυχή)** — "soul, heart, life." Verse 23 says "do it from the heart [literally *from the soul*]." This is wholehearted engagement, not grudging compliance. You bring your full self to the work—creativity, diligence, integrity—because you're working for Christ, not clocking hours for a paycheck.

- **Greek: *antapodosis* (ἀνταπόδοσις)** — "recompense, repayment, reward." This is the "inheritance" in v. 24. Paul promises future reward for faithful work. Not immediately visible. Not always acknowledged by your boss. But certain, from the Lord who sees everything. This is the long game.

**Personal Application:** This week, I'll pick one work task I've been halfhearted about—maybe documentation I've been putting off, or a code refactor I know needs doing but won't get me credit—and I'll do it as if Christ himself assigned it to me. Finish it well, commit it, and then pray Colossians 3:23 over it before moving on.

## Context & Theological Grounding

### What Colossians 3 Actually Says

Paul wrote Colossians around AD 60–62 from prison, addressing a church threatened by a hybrid false teaching—Jewish legalism mixed with proto-Gnostic mysticism and angel worship. In chapter 2, Paul dismantled the philosophy. In chapter 3, he builds the alternative: life in union with Christ.

The structure is logical:
1. **Union with Christ** (vv. 1–4) — the foundation: you've been raised with Christ; your life is hidden in him.
2. **Mortification** (vv. 5–11) — put to death the old-self patterns.
3. **Vivification** (vv. 12–17) — clothe yourself in the new-self virtues.
4. **Household faithfulness** (vv. 18–25) — work out that new identity in marriage, family, and work.

Every imperative rests on the indicative. "You died" (v. 3), "you have put off the old self" (v. 9), "God's chosen ones, holy and dearly loved" (v. 12). Paul is not saying "try harder to become someone new." He's saying "you *are* someone new—now live like it."

### How the Passages Fit Together

Colossians 3 is the practical outworking of Colossians 2's polemic. In 2:20, Paul asked, "If you died with Christ to the elements of this world, why do you live as if you still belonged to the world?" Chapter 3 is the answer: you recalibrate your mind (vv. 1–3), kill the old patterns (vv. 5–11), wear the new self (vv. 12–17), and live faithfully in your daily callings (vv. 18–25). It's comprehensive—thought life, sin patterns, character formation, and relational obedience. Nothing is left untouched.

### Reformed / Gospel Connection

This is union with Christ from start to finish. The Reformed doctrine of definitive sanctification is all over this passage: you *have been* raised with Christ (v. 1), you *have* died (v. 3), you *have* put off the old self (v. 9). The change is already real. Progressive sanctification—growing in holiness over time—is simply learning to live consistently with the definitive break that happened at conversion.

And the gospel fuels every command. Why forgive? "Just as the Lord has forgiven you" (v. 13). Why love? Because you are "dearly loved" (v. 12). Why work wholeheartedly? Because "you will receive the reward of an inheritance from the Lord" (v. 24). The Christian life is not moralism—it's responsive gratitude. Christ has already done the work; I'm living out the implications.

## Cross-References

- **Romans 6:1-14** — Paul's fullest teaching on our death with Christ and what it means to be dead to sin and alive to God.
- **Ephesians 4:17-32** — Parallel passage: put off the old self, be renewed in your mind, put on the new self.
- **Galatians 5:16-26** — The works of the flesh vs. the fruit of the Spirit—overlaps significantly with Col 3:5-14.
- **Ephesians 5:22-6:9** — Household code parallel, with more detail on Christ and the church as the marriage model.
- **1 Peter 2:18-25** — Peter's instruction to slaves/servants, grounded in Christ's example of unjust suffering.
- **Philippians 2:5-11** — The mind of Christ—humility, obedience, exaltation—as the pattern for our own mindset.
- **2 Corinthians 5:17** — "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; the old has passed away; behold, the new has come."
